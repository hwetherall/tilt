## Script

**[COLD OPEN]**

Right. First day of a World Cup, and the bookies and the maths already don't agree.

Two games tonight. The market thinks one of them is a formality and can't separate the other. I reckon the formality is the more interesting of the two — and I think a fair few people are about to lose a small, dignified amount of money being very certain about Mexico.

**[THE LAY OF THE DAY]**

Here's the card. Mexico against South Africa, hosts, home crowd, opening night. And Korea against Czechia, which on paper is about as even as football gets.

The model I follow is Nate Silver's PELE — the one that actually bothers to account for who's on the pitch, not just the badge. It's got firm opinions on both of these. Let me start with the game everyone thinks they've already solved.

But forget the win line for a second, because this is the bit that actually pays. Look at the *scoreline*. PELE's single likeliest result is one-nil. Expected goals of two for Mexico, and barely a chance of one — nought-point-four — for South Africa. That is not a procession. That's a tight, anxious, one-goal Tuesday where the hosts do just enough.

So here's my tilt, and it's not the obvious one. I'm not fading Mexico to win — the model and I agree, they're the better side. I'm fading the *manner* of it. Give me Mexico, **under two and a half goals**. If you remember one thing from me today: the hosts win the game, not the highlight reel.

The favourite *score* is nobody scoring. That tension is the entire game: two sides who'd both rather not lose than risk winning.
