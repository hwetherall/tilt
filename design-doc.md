## Problem Statement

Harry has been building Keepwatch, a Newcastle-centred Premier League season simulator with a sophisticated multi-agent LLM editorial pipeline. The 2025-26 EPL season ends Sunday, May 24 — once the season is over, the existing product becomes unusable until the 2026-27 season starts in August.

Harry wants to fill the gap (and skill-up) by building a **daily 5-10 minute World Cup 2026 podcast / YouTube show**, where a new app turns model data, betting odds, and news into a Markdown monologue he reads on camera. The 2026 FIFA World Cup kicks off **June 11, 2026** — 23 days from this session. ~40 daily episodes through the tournament window.

The fight Harry has been losing on Keepwatch is **LLM recency hallucination** — models trained on stale data confidently misstate current lineups, transfers, injuries. He's tried ESPN integration and a Supabase team-data store; "alright but not perfect."

This new product flips the constraint: Harry reads every script before recording, so he is the final factual gate. The pipeline doesn't need to be infallible.

---

## What Makes This Cool

The killer wedge, in Harry's own words:

> "Nate Silver is very good at putting together models; he's good on podcasts; he writes interesting articles, but he's not very good in television."

The bet: Nate's PELE model (Predictive Elo with Lineup Equilibria, just-launched on Silver Bulletin) is statistically excellent but commercially under-monetised in its current text-and-chart form. A charismatic English-speaking presenter reading **PELE-anchored, odds-driven daily takes** — PELE supplies the underlying thesis, the betting market supplies the daily change story — on camera each day is a separate product entirely. The independent second opinion put it sharper:

> "Harry isn't competing with Silver Bulletin — he's *arbitraging* it. Build for the arbitrage: don't try to out-model Silver, *out-perform* him. Every engineering decision should serve 'Harry on camera with a take,' not 'more accurate numbers.'"

Beyond the wedge, the daily cadence is a forcing function for Harry's stated skill-building goals (writing, presentation, coding, French v2 post-tournament). And the "for me and my mates" framing survives a YouTube public-channel layer cleanly — recording for yourself, bonus if strangers find it.

## Constraints

- **Hard timeline:** 23 days to WC kickoff (June 11, 2026). First dry-run episode target: mid-next-week. Full pipeline by kickoff.
- **Solo builder.** No team, no editor, no producer.
- **Anti-spaghetti commitment.** Direct quote from Harry: *"we're using LLM to do a ton of our coding. The thing that you end up with always is just way too much scope creep, spaghetti code, and the whole thing falls apart."* Lean, focused, ruthless about scope.
- **Keepwatch stays alive but frozen.** The existing EPL product lives on as the 2026-27 simulator. The new tool is a separate repo, separate concerns. No coupling.
- **Skill-building over automation.** No AI voice. Harry records himself. Recording/editing handled by external tools (Capcut / OBS Studio), not in-app.
- **Budget:** A few $/month for Silver Bulletin subscription (PELE projections), Odds API tier, OpenRouter/Anthropic LLM credits. No infrastructure scaling concerns at this volume.
- **Daily time budget:** End-to-end (generate → read → record → Capcut edit → upload → mate-link) target of **under 2 hours per day**. If a typical episode is taking longer, the format is too ambitious for the cadence.
- **Web-search sources are surfaced, not hidden.** Every news/injury claim in the dossier must carry a source URL Harry can click in under 5 seconds. Spot-checking 1-2 per episode is part of the read-through workflow — see Premise #3.
- **Data dependencies that are out of Harry's control:** PELE has no public API. Silver Bulletin paywall. Possible scrape ToS issues. **Fallback plan is mandatory before V1 ships — see "PELE access strategy" in Recommended Approach.**

## Premises

These are the agreed assumptions every downstream decision rests on. If any of these flip, the design must be revisited.

1. **Daily cadence, not weekly.** ~40 episodes from June 11 to July 19. Missing a day occasionally is fine; missing three is structural failure.
2. **Odds API is the daily change-detector. PELE is the weekly/episodic thesis spine.** (Revised from the original "PELE is primary" framing after second-opinion challenge: PELE updates infrequently on Silver's cadence, so it can't power a daily change narrative. Odds change every day; that's the story engine. PELE becomes the calibration anchor Harry's takes reference.)
3. **Harry is the QA gate.** He reads every script before recording. LLM hallucinations get caught by his eyes. The pipeline doesn't need to be infallible because he is.
4. **Daily output is a Markdown script with inline B-roll cues — plain-English in V1.** V1 cue format: `[B-ROLL: graphic of England-USA odds shift, last 24h]` or `[B-ROLL: PELE top 10 with tilt arrows]`. Harry sources these manually in Capcut from whatever's at hand (Silver Bulletin screenshots, Odds API charts, hand-drawn). V2 adds a B-roll dashboard whose URLs the cues will eventually point at — but V1 does *not* depend on it existing.
5. **Distribution: public YouTube channel.** WhatsApp/group-chat share to mates is bonus. Public is the forcing function for presentation skill-building.
6. **EPL Keepwatch freezes Sunday.** Site stays live as portfolio; no active maintenance until next EPL season prep in July/August.
7. **French v2 is post-tournament.** Do not try to ship bilingual before kickoff.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Fork & Repurpose the Keepwatch Roundup Pipeline
Copy `lib/weekly-roundup/` into the existing repo as `lib/daily-script/`, keep the 3-parallel-agents → editor → QA reviewer architecture, swap dossier/prompts/sections for WC.
- **Effort:** M (10-14 days)
- **Pros:** Reuses ~80% of existing infrastructure; QA reviewer catches factual errors; parallel agents keep daily wall-clock fast.
- **Cons:** Couples WC tool to Keepwatch repo (Harry's explicit concern about scope creep). Inherits EPL-shaped architecture decisions that don't fit monologue (cross-section redundancy rules, programmatic table injection).

### Approach B: LLM-as-DJ (single-call lightweight, new repo)
New repo, ~200 lines. One large-context Claude call: PELE snapshot + 24h odds diff + web search + schedule → out comes a complete Markdown script. Harry edits at read-through.
- **Effort:** S (3-5 days)
- **Pros:** Ships fastest, fast prompt iteration, clean codebase honors anti-spaghetti commitment.
- **Cons:** Less consistent voice (single shot, no editorial passes); no deterministic factual gate.

### Approach C: Pipeline + Production Studio (in existing repo)
Approach A plus a full review/edit/teleprompter web UI and a public archive.
- **Effort:** L (3-4 weeks)
- **Cons:** Won't ship by kickoff. Wrong destination first (don't know what the studio needs until you've recorded 5 episodes).

## Recommended Approach

**Tilt v1 — Lean Script Studio (new repo, single-pass generator).** Harry's chosen direction: a hybrid of B and C, deliberately rejecting both the fork-A path (couples to Keepwatch) and the production-studio piece of C (Capcut/OBS handle that).

### V1 scope (ships by June 11)

**New repo.** Separate from `road-to-europe`. Tentatively `tilt` or `pele-overwatch` — name to be confirmed.

**Stack:**
- Next.js 15+ App Router (Harry knows this; lets us add a thin script-viewing route in V1 and grow into V2's B-roll dashboard later).
- TypeScript.
- OpenRouter for LLM calls (reuse pattern from Keepwatch but rewrite clean — do not copy-paste).
- **Filesystem-first persistence** for V1: scripts saved as `/scripts/YYYY-MM-DD.md` in the repo, committed to git. Free version history, free archive, zero infra. Supabase deferred to V2 when the dashboard needs structured queries.
- Playwright for PELE page capture, **only if scrape strategy succeeds** (see PELE access strategy below). Otherwise manual paste into a JSON file.
- **Daily trigger: manual `npm run generate` at Harry's chosen morning slot.** No cron in V1. If running it manually feels painful by day 10, add a Vercel cron in V2.

**Voice spec (the script's tone):**
- British, dry, numbers-literate.
- ~900-1,200 words for a 5-10 min read at Harry's pace.
- **One strong opinion per episode** (the "tilt" of the day).
- References PELE for thesis, betting markets for what changed.
- Names sources inline ("Silver Bulletin's PELE has Brazil at 14%, the market has them at 18% — here's why I think the market is closer this week").

**Dossier JSON sketch** (the assembly target — this is what drives the prompt):
```typescript
interface DailyDossier {
  date: string;                       // ISO date
  episodeType: 'match-day' | 'preview-day' | 'rest-day' | 'knockout-gap';
  tournamentState: {
    stage: 'group' | 'r16' | 'qf' | 'sf' | 'final' | 'third-place';
    daysSinceStart: number;
    daysUntilNextMatch: number;
  };
  todaysFixtures: Array<{
    home: string; away: string; kickoffUTC: string;
    peleHomeWin: number; peleDraw: number; peleAwayWin: number;
    oddsHomeWin: number; oddsDraw: number; oddsAwayWin: number;
    delta24hPp: { home: number; draw: number; away: number };
  }>;
  peleTop10: Array<{ team: string; rating: number; tilt: number; deltaSinceYesterday: number }>;
  biggestMovers24h: Array<{ team: string; metric: string; from: number; to: number; storySeed: string }>;
  peleVsMarketDisagreements: Array<{ match: string; pelePick: string; marketPick: string; ppGap: number }>;
  newsItems: Array<{ headline: string; url: string; publishedAt: string; relevantTeams: string[] }>;  // SOURCES MANDATORY
  yesterdaysEpisodeTakes: string[];   // what Harry already said yesterday — avoid restating
}
```

**Daily generation flow:**
1. **Ingest** (3 sources):
   - PELE current ratings + projections (per access strategy below)
   - Odds API for today's WC matches + 24h delta (live)
   - Web search for last 24h news with **source URLs always returned** in the structured output
2. **Dossier assembly** (~150 lines): produce one `DailyDossier` object per above schema.
3. **Script generation: single LLM call.** Dossier + voice spec → Markdown script with `[B-ROLL: plain-English description]` cues. No second pass in V1. If output quality is shaky after the first 3 scripts, add a fact-check pass *then*, not preemptively.
4. **Persistence:** Write to `/scripts/YYYY-MM-DD.md` and `/scripts/dossiers/YYYY-MM-DD.json` in the repo. Commit.
5. **Read-through:** Harry opens `/script/[date]` route (read-only render with a "copy to clipboard" button) OR opens the .md directly in VSCode. Spot-checks at least one news-source URL. Records.

**Crucially excluded from V1:** no multi-agent orchestration, no cross-section redundancy rules, no production studio UI, no teleprompter export, no B-roll auto-rendering, no French, no auth, no signup form, no analytics, no public archive page beyond the date-route, no Keepwatch integration, **no in-app editing** (Harry edits in VSCode if needed), **no Supabase**, **no cron**, **no second-stage fact-check LLM pass**.

### PELE access strategy (with explicit fallback)

This is the highest-risk dependency. Time-box it.

1. **Subscribe step (~30 min, Next Steps #2):** Subscribe to Silver Bulletin ($10). Manually open the PELE page in a logged-in browser. Note: what's on the page, what's gated, what the HTML structure looks like, whether tables are React-rendered, whether there's Cloudflare bot protection.
2. **Spike day (one calendar day, Next Steps #3):** Time-boxed Playwright spike — try to log in and capture the ratings/projections table. **Hard stop at end of the day.**
3. **If scrape works by end of spike day:** wire as data source for the dossier. Capture once daily; cache as JSON.
4. **If scrape doesn't work by end of spike day:** **Fall back to manual paste.** Harry copies the relevant PELE numbers into `/data/pele-snapshot.json` once a day (5-10 min effort) before running `npm run generate`. Sustainable for 40 days and removes all scrape-fragility risk.


**Decision rule:** the show ships even if PELE access fails entirely. The Odds API + web search + Harry's takes is the minimum viable product. PELE is a credibility amplifier, not a load-bearing dependency.

### Web-search hallucination defence

The original recency problem doesn't disappear by switching to web search — it changes shape. Mitigations baked into V1:
- Web-search structured output must return `{headline, url, publishedAt, relevantTeams[]}` per item. URLs are non-negotiable.
- The script-generation prompt explicitly forbids stating injury/squad facts that don't trace to a dossier news item. If an LLM tries to add one, the deterministic dossier-render skips it.
- Read-through workflow: Harry clicks at least one news URL per episode to spot-check before recording.

### V2 scope (post-kickoff, during tournament's quieter days)

Once V1 is shipping daily episodes, the V2 add is a **B-roll dashboard** — clean charts and visualisations Harry can screenshot for the video. Built fresh, not copy-pasted from Keepwatch's 1,656-line Dashboard.tsx. Likely components:
- PELE-vs-market scatter (which teams the market and PELE disagree on)
- 24h odds-shift bar chart
- A "today's matches" panel with PELE win probs and tilt arrows
- A bracket / group-stage state view

V2 is explicitly *deferred* until V1 has produced at least 5 episodes — by then Harry will know what visuals he actually wants instead of guessing.

### What we keep from Keepwatch

Patterns, not code:
- The OpenRouter wrapper and structured-output schemas (study, then rewrite clean)
- The web-search → research-bundle pattern (much simpler version for V1)
- The Supabase audit / cache pattern (much simpler version for V1)

What we don't copy: the orchestrator, the section-agent multi-wave system, the validator + quality-checks + reviewer pipeline. Those were built for weekly editorial QA. Harry-in-the-loop replaces them.

For the tournament window (June 11 – July 19):
- **30+ episodes published** (allowing some missed days; food-poisoning happens).
- A consistent voice across episodes that mate-feedback confirms ("sounds like Harry").
- At least one episode where a "market vs PELE" disagreement is the day's hook.

Out-of-scope success criteria (deliberately):
- Subscriber count, watch time, comment engagement. Vanity metrics for V1; the bar is "shipped daily and the tapes feel good."

4. **Wire Odds API for World Cup fixtures.** Confirm WC coverage. Build the 24h-delta query. (2-3 hours)
5. **Wire the web-search integration with mandatory source URLs.** Structured output schema rejects items without URLs. (2-3 hours)
6. **Build dossier assembly module.** PELE + Odds + news → one `DailyDossier` JSON matching the schema in Recommended Approach. (~half a day)
7. **Write the script-generation prompt.** This is the entire product. Iterate on a sample dossier (real recent international match, e.g. last week's UEFA Nations League fixture). Test 5-10 prompt variants. (1 full day)
8. **Wire the `/script/[date]` route + filesystem persistence + copy-to-clipboard button.** (half a day)
9. **Generate one full script for a hypothetical WC opening-day fixture.** Read it yourself out loud — does it sound like you? Iterate the prompt if not. (half a day)
